Abstract
Promptable video trackers such as SAM2 achieve accurate
and robust performance through strong temporal consis-
tency, making them important foundations for downstream
video understanding and tracking applications.
As their
use continues to expand, understanding their robustness
to adversarial perturbations becomes increasingly impor-
tant.
Existing attacks on SAM2 largely focus on local
mask degradation and short-term temporal feature disrup-
tion, leaving its longer-term dynamics and competing ob-
ject interferences (Distractors) insufficiently explored. We
observe that SAM2 itself exposes useful distractor cues
when tracking becomes uncertain.
Based on these ob-
servations, we propose DistractAttack, a white-box attack
that uses background distractors to disrupt target tracking
persistently. Its core procedure, Counterfactual Distrac-
tor Search (CDS), propagates clean and attacked trajec-
tories in parallel to discover plausible competing regions
and identify those that become increasingly effective un-
der perturbation. The discovered evidence is accumulated
over time to weaken target support and promote recur-
rent alternatives or disappearance predictions. Extensive
experiments under video-level and one-shot perturbations
across Visual Object Tracking and Video Object Segmenta-
tion demonstrate strong attack effectiveness, raising DRE to
4.4× and NRE to 7.3× their nearest baselines under video
perturbation, while transferring effectively across model
scales and tracker variants in black-box settings. Project
page: https://adonaydem.github.io/sam2-
da-page/.

We introduce DistractAttack, a distractor-centric white-
box attack for recurrent promptable segmentation that uses
model-supported distractors to destabilize target tracking
rather than merely erase the current mask. Its core, Coun-
terfactual Distractor Search (CDS), propagates clean and
attacked trajectories in parallel: the clean trajectory iden-
tifies plausible non-target competitors, while the attacked
trajectory reveals which become increasingly influential un-
der a bounded ℓ∞perturbation. Accumulating this evidence
over time, CDS turns local ambiguity into persistent track-
ing failures, including wrong-object capture, spatial dis-

evaluate video-level and one-shot perturbations across VOT
and VOS, with transfer studies across model scales and
state-of-the-art tracker variants. SAM2’s memory mech-
anism is particularly vulnerable to one-shot initialization
perturbations, whose effects persist throughout the video.
Under video-perturbation and matched protocol, Distrac-
tAttack achieves the highest Drift-Rate Error (DRE) and

2. Related Work
2.1. Promptable Video Segmentation and Tracking
SAM introduced promptable class-agnostic image segmen-
tation [22], and SAM2 extended this paradigm to video
through a streaming memory that propagates an initial-
ized object across frames [33]. SAM2 further broadened
the family toward concept-prompted detection, segmenta-
tion, and tracking [3]. Recent SAM2 extensions strengthen
long-term tracking through motion-aware memory selec-
tion in SAMURAI [42], multiple temporal hypotheses in
SAM2Long [9], distractor-aware memory in DAM4SAM
[37], position prompting in SAMITE [41], and combined
long- and short-term modeling in HiM2SAM [4].
Their
shared focus on distractors and memory reliability identi-
fies identity confusion as a central challenge in promptable
tracking. We study the complementary adversarial question
of whether a bounded perturbation can exploit such distrac-
tors to progressively confuse the tracker’s target representa-
tion and disrupt its temporal consistency.
2.2. Adversarial Video Tracking and Segmentation
Adversarial video tracking has shown that recurrence, as-
sociation, and stored representations can turn brief pertur-
bations into persistent errors.
In visual object tracking,
FAN, IoU Attack, SPARK, CSA, and ABA attack tempo-
ral box consistency, sparse online updates, classification re-
sponses, and motion blur [5, 13, 14, 18, 25], while adver-
sarial VOS exploits initialization errors that aim to induce
mask degradation [19, 24]. However, these frameworks re-
main closely tied to their victim architectures and have lim-
ited compatibility with recent SAM-based trackers. Attacks
on SAM have covered mask removal, targeted generation,
prompt-agnostic feature manipulation, region suppression
and spatial–frequency attacks [15, 17, 20, 26, 28, 32, 35,
38, 39, 43, 45, 46]. Direct studies of SAM2 include Ro-
bust SAM (CPA), UAP-SAM2, BadVSFM, and DarkLLM
[27, 36, 44, 47]. Among these methods, UAP-SAM2 alone
introduces an explicit temporal objective, yet it merely re-
lates consecutive encoder features rather than the history
accumulated in recurrent memory. We aim to exploit ac-
cumulated target history and recurring distractor evidence
to induce persistent confusion between the target and plau-
sible competing regions.

Together, these observations motivate Counterfactual
Distractor Search (CDS), which separates evidence collec-
tion from perturbation optimization. A clean trajectory pro-
vides target features and competing regions, while an at-
tacked trajectory reveals how distractor evidence evolves
under the bounded perturbation.
Optimization then sup-
presses target support, biases foreground responses toward
discovered distractors, and corrupts target distinctiveness
across multiple granularities.
We consider two settings.
Video Perturbation (VP) optimizes one perturbation per
video, while One-shot Perturbation (OP) perturbs only
the prompted frame [6, 19].
DistractAttack is primarily
classified as untargeted because no distractor identity is
specified in advance. Its distractor proposals guide the opti-
mization but may instead exhibit wrong-object capture, spa-
tial displacement, unstable switching, or disappearance.

6. Ablation Studies
Unless varied, both settings use 10 optimization passes,
with ϵ = 8/255 for VP and ϵ = 4/255 for OP. The default
OP rollout uses N = 20 frames.
Across the attack objectives, mask suppression weakens
both tasks but induces little drift.
Displacement (Ldisp)
redirects this failure toward spatial misdirection, while clut-
ter corruption (Lclut) strengthens the attack across both
tasks.
Their combination reduces Q to 0.420 under VP
and 0.076 under OP. Removing mask suppression raises
OP DRE but is substantially weaker in Q and VOS Avg.
For iteration length, ten passes substantially outperform
five in both settings. Fifteen further improves VP but gains
only 0.005 in OP Q while worsening DRE and VOS per-
formance. We therefore retain ten passes. With increasing
perturbation magnitude, a larger budget strengthens the
attack but permits more visible perturbations. Both Q and
VOS Avg. decrease with ϵ, while VP DRE peaks at the se-
lected 8/255. Across sources of distractor evidence, se-
mantic proposals provide broad coverage, while alternative
masks and adversarial attention maps add decoder-specific
cues. Removing any source weakens both tasks, and every
reduced variant remains behind full CDS. For historical
banks and the update policy, restricting E to the first or
current frame weakens the attack. The current-frame vari-
ant applies only to VP because OP optimizes the initializa-
tion frame alone. FIFO replaces entries by arrival order,
whereas uniform updates ignore consistency with existing
distractors, so neither effectively preserves or prioritizes re-
curring evidence. Full CDS performs best by maintaining
a persistent E and prioritizing proposals supported by its
most effective entries. Supporting ablations are given in
Appendix 9.4.

Defense Case Study. We evaluate complementary feature-
and memory-level defenses against DistractAttack. Robust-
SAM [27] adversarially adapts the singular values of
selected convolutional weights.
We further repurpose
SAM2Long’s attention modulation [9] to attenuate low-
confidence memory keys and limit uncertain time steps and
initialization errors. Robust-SAM and our memory defense
raise Q from 0.420 to 0.515 and 0.476, respectively. Com-
bined, they reach 0.532 Q and reduce DRE from 10.83% to
8.45%, but remain well below the clean Q of 0.836. Exist-
ing feature and memory controls therefore offer only partial
protection. Details are provided in Appendix 10.
7. Conclusion
We examined how adversarial perturbations compromise
target identity in SAM2. Although recurrent memory pro-
motes temporal consistency, it can propagate perturbation-
induced local ambiguity into persistent tracking failure. We
introduced DistractAttack, a distractor-centric attack that
exploits plausible competing regions rather than relying
solely on foreground suppression. Its Counterfactual Dis-
tractor Search jointly follows clean and attacked trajecto-
ries, identifies model-supported distractors, and accumu-
lates their evidence over time to sustain failure.
Across
VOT and VOS, DistractAttack remains effective under
both perturbation settings and long temporal horizons,
while generalizing and transferring across model scales and
tracker variants. These results identify distractor-induced
failure and initialization sensitivity as key vulnerabilities
of SAM2-based trackers, motivating stronger robustness
mechanisms in video foundation models.


Broader Societal Impact
This work provides new insight into the vulnerabilities of
SAM2. We hope these findings can inform future efforts
to better understand and mitigate such failure modes, con-
tributing to safer and more reliable deployment.

8. Implementation Details
This section gives the settings used to reproduce Distrac-
tAttack. We follow the VP and OP definitions in Sec. 4.
The victim model and its tracker state remain differentiable,
while all model parameters are frozen.
8.1. Attack Optimization
Shared setup. We use the official pretrained SAM 2.1-L
checkpoint [33]. Frames follow the official SAM 2 prepro-
cessing and are resized to 1024 × 1024. The perturbation
is initialized from δ ∼U(−ϵ, ϵ), with ϵ defaulting to 8/255
and 4/255, for VP and OP respectively, and updated with
the projected signed gradient rule in Eq. (18). After every
update, δ is projected onto the ℓ∞ball of radius ϵ. When
composing an attacked frame, x + δ is clipped to [0, 1] be-
fore normalization.
Video perturbation. VP optimizes one perturbation for
each video. The same perturbation is applied to every frame
in its fixed window.
Clean and attacked trajectories are
propagated together during each pass. Their detached banks
provide the temporal context used by the attack losses.
One-shot perturbation. OP changes only the prompted
frame.
Later frames remain clean and provide evidence
for the target and effective-distractor banks.
The clutter
objective acts on the perturbed frame 0 encoder features,
while later clean frames contribute detached evidence to the
banks. Neither setting uses ground truth beyond the initial-
ization prompt during optimization.
8.2. CDS Configuration
Banks. The target bank stores at most 20 foreground and
background prototype pairs. Later entries require predicted-
IoU and objectness scores above 0.5 and 0, respectively.
We use a novelty threshold of τnov = 0.98. These checks
prevent closely repeated observations from filling the bank.
The effective-distractor bank has capacity K = 10.
Its
merge threshold is θmerge = 0.85 and its EMA rate is
η = 0.1. We set the routing temperature to τw = 0.10
and the clutter-loss contrastive temperature to τc = 0.07.
Loss.
A grid search over {0.25, 0.5, 1.0} for each loss
weight selected λM = λD = λC = 1.0. The global and
local clutter weights are ηG = 1.0 and ηL = 1.0.
8.3. Datasets and Metrics
Visual object tracking. The VOT set contains 104 ran-
domly sampled sequences from LaSOT [10], UTB180 [2],
GOT-10k [16], and LaSOT-ext [10].
The LaSOT and
UTB180 subset follows the distractor-distilled DiDi selec-
tion [37].
GOT-10k and LaSOT-ext add broader track-
ing conditions including videos with no distractors. Each
source dataset contributes 22-31 randomly sampled se-
quences. Each sequence contributes a long 100-frame win-
dow, in contrast to 15 frame window used by published
UAP-SAM2. None of these evaluation splits was used to
train the original SAM 2 model.
Video object segmentation. The VOS set contains 100
videos. We sample 25 videos from each source and se-
lect one target object. DAVIS 2017 [31] provides accurate
boundaries. YouTube-VOS 2018 validation [40] adds broad
appearance and category variation. The MOSEv2 [8] con-
tains crowded scenes and severe occlusion. SA-V test [33]
covers motion and disappearance. Similarily, each video is
100 frame long. None of these evaluation splits was used to
train the original SAM 2 model.

8.4. Baseline Adaptations
Common protocol. We retain each baseline’s defining ob-
jective and apply it under the same VP or OP constraint
as DistractAttack. VP aggregates each method’s native loss
over the sequence. OP perturbs only the initialization frame.
When a method requires a reference after frame 0, we use
detached clean predictions rather than future annotations.
All methods share the same preprocessing, prompts, tem-
poral windows, random seeds, ℓ∞bounds.
Image-based attacks. We adapt AttackSAM, DarkSAM,
CPA, SegPGD, and UAD from static image segmenta-
tion to the recurrent setting. AttackSAM [43] applies its
ClipMSE objective to the selected SAM 2 mask predic-
tion. DarkSAM [46] retains its semantic-decoupling and
wavelet-texture terms. CPA [27] retains its negative-feature
objective over the top responses shared by point and box
prompts. SegPGD [12] treats each SAM 2 output as binary
segmentation, uses the detached clean mask as its pseudo-
label, and retains its iteration-dependent weighting sched-
ule.
UAD [28] preserves its two-stage feature objective
with deformation fields. VP aggregates this objective across
the rollout, while OP applies it to the initialization entering
memory.
UAP-SAM2. UAP-SAM2 [47] combines target scanning,
semantic confusion, encoder feature shift, and temporal fea-
ture misalignment. We retain these terms while changing
setting to VP and OP.

10. Defense Study
The main paper tests a feature-level defense and a memory-
level defense. We describe both adaptations here. The at-
tack remains white box and is recomputed against each de-
fended model.
10.1. Robust-SAM
Robust-SAM [27] adapts the singular values of selected
convolutional weights. We apply this parameterization to
the convolutional weights in the final image-encoder block
of SAM 2.1-L. Each selected weight is decomposed as
W = UPV ⊤, where U and V remain frozen and only the
diagonal matrix P is optimized. All other SAM 2 parame-
ters remain fixed.
We train a separate defended model for each perturba-
tion setting using CPA adversarial examples generated from
5,000 videos in the SA-V training split [33]. The training
bound matches the corresponding evaluation setting, with
ϵ = 8/255 for VP and ϵ = 4/255 for OP. Each model is
adapted for 15 epochs using a batch size of 64 and Adam
with a learning rate of 10−3. Thus, the VP and OP results
use independently adapted checkpoints rather than a single
jointly trained defense.
