Towards Deep Learning-Based Linear Assignment
Problem Solvers for Multi-Object Tracking
Chu Tam Vu†, Tran Thi Thuy Quynh†, and Do Hai Son‡∗.
† VNU University of Engineering and Technology, Hanoi, Vietnam.
‡ School of Electrical Engineering, Computing and Mathematical Sciences, Curtin University, Australia.
Abstract—This paper proposes and evaluates deep learning
(DL)-based linear assignment problem (LAP) solvers as a re-
placement for the classical Hungarian algorithm in multi-object
tracking (MOT) systems. Three representative neural models
are studied, namely a convolutional network (CNN), a graph
neural network (GNN), and the graph-based linear assignment
network (GLAN). A cost-matrix embedding is introduced so
that a single model operates on cost matrices of any size, as
required in MOT where the number of objects varies over
time, without training a separate model for each input shape.
Extensive simulations show that the embedding improves the
accuracy of the neural solvers (i.e., CNN and GNN), while GLAN
attains the best accuracy among them. These solvers are then
integrated into a generalized labeled multi-Bernoulli (GLMB)
tracker and evaluated on the WILDTRACK dataset, where
GLAN yields tracking accuracy close to the Hungarian reference
and comparable to recent DL-based trackers.
Index Terms—Linear assignment problem, deep learning,
multi-object tracking.
I. INTRODUCTION
Multi-object tracking (MOT) estimates the trajectories of a
time-varying number of objects, and it underpins applications
such as intelligent surveillance, autonomous driving, and mo-
bile robotics [1], [2]. At each time step, an MOT system de-
tects the objects and maintains their identities. The latter relies
on data association, which assigns the measurements from a
sensor to the objects being tracked [3]. Tracking frameworks
such as the generalized labeled multi-Bernoulli (GLMB) filters
perform this association through multiple hypotheses to esti-
mate object trajectories over time [4]. Each such association is
commonly formulated as a linear assignment problem (LAP)
that seeks a one-to-one (1-1) mapping between the two sets at
minimum total cost.
Classical LAP solvers obtain the exact solution through
combinatorial optimization, including the Hungarian algo-
rithm [5], [6], the auction algorithm [7], and shortest
augmenting-path methods [8]. The Hungarian and shortest
augmenting-path methods return a globally optimal mapping
with a computational complexity of O(N 3), and their opti-
mized implementations run fast at moderate problem sizes.
This cubic cost nevertheless grows quickly with the numbers
of objects and measurements and becomes a bottleneck in
large-scale or high-density tracking scenarios. Faster subop-
timal alternatives, such as greedy assignment [9], lower the

cost by committing to locally favorable matches at the expense
of the global optimality guarantee. However, both exact and
greedy solvers are also discrete and non-differentiable, which
limits their use in gradient-based learning and has motivated
differentiable relaxations of the assignment. The Sinkhorn
procedure alternately normalizes the rows and columns of
a positive matrix to approximate a permutation by a dou-
bly stochastic matrix [10], and entropy-regularized optimal
transport and Gumbel-Sinkhorn extend this idea to differen-
tiable optimization and latent permutation learning [11], [12].
These methods produce soft assignments that still require a
discretization step to yield a valid mapping.
Recently, deep learning (DL) networks have been used
to solve the LAP [13], replacing the iterative combinatorial
search with a single forward pass. Additionally, their contin-
uous output is differentiable with respect to the costs. Hence,
the DL-based Hungarian has been exploited to train end-to-
end (E2E) DL-based trackers [14]. Lee et al. [15] first cast the
assignment of a measurement to each object as a classification
problem solved by a convolutional network (CNN) that takes
the cost matrix as a two-dimensional input. In 2024, Aironi
et al. [16] represent the LAP as a bipartite graph and apply
a graph neural network (GNN), and Liu et al. [17] propose
the graph-based linear assignment network (GLAN), which
learns assignment decisions from edge representations. An
MOT system requires a solver that operates on cost matrices of
varying size, since the numbers of objects and measurements
change over time. However, the CNN [15] and GNN [16]
assume a pre-defined cost-matrix shape and cannot accept such
variable-size inputs. GLAN, in contrast, operates on the graph
of the current matrix and already accommodates any size.
To address the limitation of CNN and GNN methods, this
paper introduces a cost-matrix embedding that maps any cost
matrix whose dimensions do not exceed a target size N into
a common N × N representation. Thus, networks with a pre-
defined input shape (i.e., CNN and GNN) are trained once
and then applied to the variable-size problems that arise in
tracking. The embedding pads a rectangular cost matrix to a
common dimension before inference and projects the predic-
tion back to the original size, leaving the network architecture
unchanged. The three DL-based solvers, with the embedding
applied to CNN and GNN, are studied under variable-size LAP
settings and compared against the Hungarian algorithm on a
large synthetic dataset in terms of assignment accuracy and

inference time. All three solvers are then integrated into a
GLMB tracker [18] on the real-world WILDTRACK dataset,
where they replace the Hungarian assignment solver. The main
contributions are as follows:
• A cost-matrix embedding that enables neural LAP solvers
restricted to a single input size to process cost matrices
of varying size.
• Extensive simulations on synthetic LAP instances, to-

WILDTRACK dataset.

II. BACKGROUND
This section reviews the LAP formulation and the Hungar-
ian algorithm used to generate the ground-truth assignments.
A. Linear assignment problem
In MOT systems, data association is an LAP between the
set of objects and the set of measurements. Each candidate
measurement-to-object pair carries a cost, and a solution is a
1-1 mapping θ in which every object and every measurement
appear in at most one pair. With m objects and n mea-
surements, such a mapping is feasible if it contains exactly
min(m, n) pairs, and we denote by Θ the set of all feasible
mappings.
For the square case m = n = N, consider a cost matrix
C ∈RN×N, where cij denotes the cost of assigning the i-th
object (in the existing set of objects) to the j-th measurement
(in the set of measurements). A mapping θ is represented by
a binary assignment matrix X ∈{0, 1}N×N, where

The Hungarian algorithm is one of the most widely used
exact solvers for the LAP [5], [6]. Rather than searching over
all permutations, it exploits the primal-dual structure of the
assignment problem. Let ui and vj denote the dual variables

computational complexity of O(N 3).
In this work, the Hungarian algorithm generates the ground-
truth Y for each cost matrix C, and the neural networks are
trained to approximate the mapping from C to Y.

Each model resolves the conflicts in bYR with its own greedy

flicting rows are greedily reassigned to unused columns
in order of increasing cost [15].
• GNN: For a row selected by several columns, the pair

B. A real-world tracking problem
1) Experimental setup: The three solvers are integrated into
a GLMB tracker [18] and evaluated on the WILDTRACK
dataset [19], which contains 7 synchronized cameras observing
the same crowded outdoor area. The 3DROM detector [20]
fuses the per-camera detections into ground-plane (x, y) coor-
dinates. We use the standard linear Gaussian constant-velocity
model in [21], with a kinematic state of planar position and
velocity and measurements that are noisy observations of
the position. The process and measurement noise standard
deviations are set to 0.15 m/s2 and 0.1 m, respectively. Since
3DROM is trained on the first 90% of the sequence, every
tracker in Tab. III is evaluated on the final 40 frames, which
form the remaining 10%. The GLMB filter produces the likeli-
hood scores that define the measurement-to-object assignment
cost, and all four GLMB trackers share the same detections
and filter configuration, so they differ only in the solver applied
to these costs. The costs vary in size across frames. For

V. CONCLUSIONS
This paper studied DL-based LAP solvers as a replacement
for the Hungarian algorithm in MOT. A cost-matrix embedding
was introduced so that a single model handles cost matrices
of any size up to its target dimension, and CNN, GNN,
and GLAN were evaluated on synthetic LAP instances and
within a GLMB tracker on WILDTRACK. The embedding
improved the accuracy of CNN and GNN at small and medium
sizes, GLAN attained the best accuracy among the three, and,
within the tracker, GLAN approached the Hungarian reference
while the neural solvers ran faster than the pure Python
implementation. These results demonstrate the feasibility and
potential of DL-based LAP solvers for tracking. Future work
will explore other neural models to improve the assignment
accuracy, accelerate the post-processing to reach the speed of
optimized exact solvers, and exploit the differentiable score
matrix to train end-to-end trackers.
