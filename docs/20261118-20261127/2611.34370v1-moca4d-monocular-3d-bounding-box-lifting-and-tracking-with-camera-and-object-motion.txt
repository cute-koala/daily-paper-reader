Abstract
Monocular 3D bounding box tracking must account for both
object motion and changes in camera viewpoint. Framewise
box lifting with temporal filtering leaves these motion compo-
nents implicit, while static-scene fusion assumes stationary
objects. We introduce MoCA4D, a framework for 3D box
lifting and tracking from monocular video, associated 2D
box prompts, and camera intrinsics. An observation branch
estimates per-frame geometry, while a dynamic branch pre-
dicts a camera transformation shared across objects and a
separate rigid transformation for each object. We propose
Star Optimization to refine current depth and orientation us-
ing the observation and historical boxes propagated through
these transformations. The refined box then guides subse-
quent propagation. Across all evaluated objects in Argoverse
2 and ADT, Star Optimization reduces center motion error
by 38.1% and 38.7%, respectively, relative to Kalman fil-
tering of the same observations. On MOVi-F, the dynamic
branch achieves the lowest corner and rotation errors and
the highest IoU among the evaluated methods over eight-
transition motion windows. Real-data camera-only controls
further link predicted object motion to improved localization
on dynamic-object tracks.
1. Introduction
As illustrated in Fig. 1, MoCA4D estimates object-centric
4D geometry from monocular video, predicting 3D position,
orientation, and dimensions over time, alongside object and

camera SE(3) motions. These quantities support robotic
manipulation [18, 30, 47], dynamic-scene simulation for au-
tonomous driving [16, 27], and viewpoint and object-motion
control in video generation [11, 34]. Box trajectories also
provide compact controls for character animation [3, 44].
Monocular image 3D detectors [6, 19, 20, 43, 46] esti-
mate per-frame object geometry without explicitly modeling
motion across frames. Their tracking extensions, such as
WildDet3D [19], apply Kalman filtering and rotation smooth-
ing to framewise boxes without separately estimating camera
and object motion. Video-based methods [9, 17] integrate
observations across time to estimate temporally consistent
3D geometry. However, their temporal formulations are built
on the assumption that objects remain static in the world
frame.
MoCA4D operates in per-frame camera coordinates, esti-
mating 3D bounding boxes and separate camera and object
SE(3) motions through two branches sharing a visual back-
bone. The observation branch uses a box-conditioned Dense
Prediction Transformer (DPT) [31] and a regression head to
predict geometry from a 2D prompt. The dynamic branch
processes consecutive frames with correspondence features
and object-conditioned spatial tokens. Dedicated camera
and object queries aggregate information from these features
through attention. Separate regression heads then map the
updated camera queries to a shared camera SE(3) transforma-
tion and the updated object queries to object-specific SE(3)
transformations.
We propose Star Optimization to connect the two
branches through motion-guided temporal refinement. It
propagates historical boxes into the current camera frame
and combines them with the current observation through
closed-form depth and orientation updates. The refined box
then feeds back into subsequent propagation.
Our contributions are as follows:
• We introduce MoCA4D, a monocular 3D box lifting and
tracking framework that jointly predicts object geometry,
shared camera motion, and object-specific rigid motion.
• We propose Star Optimization, a closed-form refinement
of current depth and orientation using the observation and
motion-propagated historical boxes, with recursive reuse
of the refined state.
• MoCA4D achieves efficient box lifting and accurate rigid-
motion propagation, while Star Optimization reduces
tracking motion errors relative to filtering the same ob-
servations. Controlled ablations examine motion decom-
position, supervision, and temporal refinement.
2. Related Work
3D Geometry Prediction.
3D bounding box prediction
estimates an object’s position, orientation, and dimensions,
providing a compact representation of its geometry. Cube
R-CNN [6] and OVMono3D [43] recover these parameters
from object region features, while DetAny3D [46] uses a
prompt-conditioned transformer. MoCA3D [20] formulates
box prediction in the image plane, estimating projected cor-
ners and their depths through dense heatmaps and depth
maps. Other representations include normalized object coor-
dinates [38] and meshes [13], which encode canonical sur-
face coordinates and object shape, respectively. MoCA4D
adopts 3D bounding boxes in its observation branch, provid-
ing competitive geometric accuracy and efficient inference
for subsequent motion estimation and tracking.
Object Motion Estimation.
Rigid object motion can be
recovered from 3D point trajectories or estimated through
6DoF pose tracking. Point-tracking methods [2, 45] and
4D reconstruction models [10, 21, 28, 36] recover temporal
3D correspondences, from which object transformation can
be obtained through fitting. SM4RT [25] explicitly models
structured scene motion using rigid motion bases. For 6DoF
tracking, FoundationPose [40] uses RGB-D observations
with a CAD model, while BundleSDF [39] jointly tracks and
reconstructs unknown objects from RGB-D video. MoCA4D
directly predicts camera and object SE(3) transformations
from monocular video. This object-level parameterization
preserves cuboid rigidity during propagation.
3D Bounding Box Tracking.
DetAny4D [17] jointly esti-
mates 3D boxes and camera poses and promotes temporal
consistency in a shared world coordinate system. Boxer [9]
lifts 2D prompts to 3D boxes and integrates them through
tracking and fusion for static scene reconstruction. Wild-
Det3D [19] filters framewise boxes with Kalman filter-
ing [23] and quaternion smoothing. MoCA4D explicitly
predicts object-specific rigid motion separately from the
camera transformation, using their composition to propagate
historical boxes before refining the current estimate.

3.2. Observation Branch
The observation branch estimates a 3D bounding box for
each object in the current frame. As shown in Fig. 2, we ex-
tract features from four intermediate layers of the DA3 back-
bone [24] and fuse them using a box-conditioned DPT [31].
Following the box-conditioning design of MoCA3D [20],
we inject a spatial prior encoding positions relative to the 2D
box and apply cross-attention [37] between image features
and box embeddings.
We adopt a query decoder inspired by WildDet3D [19].
For each box prompt, a learnable query attends to the en-
coded prompt and the fused DPT features. The query is
further conditioned on camera-ray embeddings computed
from Kt, together with features from the final DA3 layer.
The resulting query feeds separate box and confidence
heads. The box head predicts the projected center as an
offset from the prompt center, depth, dimensions, and a 6D
rotation representation. Given the predicted projected center

3.5. Training Datasets and Settings
We train the two branches independently with a frozen
DA3-Large [24] backbone. Observation pretraining uses
Omni3D [6]; dynamic pretraining uses MOVi-F [14]. We
adapt both on HOT3D [4], HOI4D [26], AV2 [41], and
Virtual KITTI 2 [7]. Appendix A describes the feature repre-
sentations, supervision, and training schedules.
Star Optimization requires no training. We set wo = 0.25,
λc = 0.5, and λR = 1, with center weights (1/7, 2/7, 4/7)
from oldest to newest and uniform rotation weights.

4. Experiments
4.1. Experimental Setup
Baselines.
For box lifting,
we compare with De-
tAny3D [46], OVMono3D-Lift [43], and WildDet3D [19].
For object motion, we use SM4RT [25], ARROW [10],
Point4D [21], 4RC [28], V-DPM [36], L4P [2], and
TAPIP3D [45] with Depth Anything 3 [24]. Tracking com-
pares framewise boxes, causal Kalman filtering [23] with
rotation smoothing, and Star Optimization. Our filtering
adaptation of WildDet3D uses actual elapsed time and ex-
cludes future-track corrections. We also evaluate Boxer’s [9]
framewise lifting with gravity. Since its tracking and fu-
sion assume static objects in world coordinates, we use its
per-frame lifting outputs to evaluate trajectories that include
moving objects.
Datasets and inputs.
Observation accuracy is evaluated on
Omni3D [6], rigid motion on MOVi-F [14], and tracking on
150 AV2 validation recordings [41] and 10 ADT Apartment
recordings [29]. ADT is an external evaluation dataset used
neither for pretraining nor adaptation of either MoCA4D
branch. For Omni3D, we follow the MoCA3D [20] pre-
processing protocol, using tight 2D box annotations supple-
mented with boxes derived from SAM2 [32] where tight
boxes are unavailable. AV2 uses SAM 2-derived pseudo
boxes, while ADT uses ground-truth 2D boxes. For box lift-
ing and tracking, all methods receive the same associated 2D
prompts and provided camera intrinsics. These experiments
evaluate geometry and 3D trajectories conditional on the
prompts, without object detection or identity association.
4.2. Monocular 3D Bounding Box Estimation
We report PAGuv [20], the mean pixel error of projected 3D
box corners after Hungarian matching at a 512-pixel refer-
ence resolution, and 3D IoU. In Table 1, MoCA4D achieves
the lowest PAGuv on ARKitScenes [5] and SUNRGBD [35]
and the second-lowest overall on Omni3D, with competitive
IoU on the two indoor datasets. It processes 11.52 obser-
vations per second, providing 1.7× the throughput of the
fastest baseline with the lowest GPU memory footprint of
1.65 GiB.

5. Conclusion
We introduce MoCA4D for monocular 3D bounding box
lifting and tracking with explicit camera and object motion.
Star Optimization connects these predictions over time by
fusing current observations with propagated history, enabling
causal tracking of moving objects. Experiments demonstrate
efficient box lifting and more accurate center localization
than the external Kalman baselines on dynamic-object tracks
in AV2 and ADT. MoCA4D also yields lower center motion
errors on both datasets and lower rotation motion errors on
AV2.
MoCA4D relies on associated 2D box prompts, leaving
object detection outside its scope. Its rigid motion formula-

tion captures each object’s overall movement but does not
model internal articulation or deformation.

A. Implementation and training details
Both branches share a frozen DA3-Large-1.1 image en-
coder [24] with 24 transformer blocks. The encoder takes
a 518 × 518 RGB frame as input. It divides the frame into
non-overlapping 14 × 14-pixel patches, forming a 37 × 37
grid. For the current frame, the observation branch reads
1,024-dimensional patch features from blocks 12, 16, 20, and
24. The dynamic branch reads the block 12 patch features
from each of two consecutive frames. The shared encoder
accounts for 304.37M of the 396.35M model parameters.
We train the observation branch (56.78M) and the dynamic
branch (35.20M) separately.
A.1. Observation architecture and supervision
The observation branch reads 1,024-channel features from
encoder blocks 12, 16, 20 and 24. We adapt DA3’s multi-
frame design to MoCA4D by extracting these features from
each frame independently. A box-conditioned DPT fuses
these features into a 129×129 spatial grid with 256 channels.
The prompt encoder has three layers of width 256. For each
prompted object, one query passes through two decoder lay-
ers, each with eight attention heads and a feed-forward width
of 2,048. Each layer has its own geometry and confidence
heads.
We supervise the encoded center offset, depth, dimen-
sions, and rotation with ℓ1 losses, and train the confidence
head using binary cross-entropy with soft quality targets
based on depth accuracy and box overlap. Supervision is
applied to every decoder layer:

C. Evaluation data and protocols
C.1. Datasets and splits
ADT is an external evaluation dataset used neither for pre-
training nor adaptation of either MoCA4D branch. AV2
is included in branch adaptation and is evaluated on its
validation recordings. Table S1 summarizes the training
roles of the principal evaluation datasets; the complete
training configuration is given in Sec. A. AV2 prompts are
SAM 2 [32]-derived pseudo boxes, while ADT prompts are
dataset ground-truth 2D boxes.
For the conditioning ablation (Table 4), all variants share
the initialization, frozen backbone, seed, 60,000-object train-
ing subset and 2,000 updates. We average within each of
the six validation datasets and then across datasets (10,523
objects total).
C.2. Inputs for motion estimation and tracking
MOVi-F motion baselines.
For MOVi-F, point and recon-
struction baselines receive a first-frame GT mask for point
selection and a first-frame GT depth scale held fixed across
objects and resets. Offline variants access future frames;
causal variants access current and past frames. TAPIP3D
uses DA3-predicted depth and intrinsics. These adaptations
differ from the prompt-conditioned trajectory task.
Tracking protocol.
We evaluate 150 AV2 validation
recordings and 10 ADT Apartment recordings on common
prepared object–time support. The final scored support con-
tains 69,434 box events and 60,119 motion pairs on AV2,
and 431,681 box events and 401,980 motion pairs on ADT.
Observations are acquired every two raw frames; the dy-
namic branch also consumes intermediate frames. Scoring
starts after four observations are available. Tracks are initial-
ized from predicted boxes and receive no ground-truth resets.
We average within each object, then across objects within a
recording, and finally across recordings within each dataset.
When Star cannot compute a valid refinement, it returns the
current 3D box predicted by the observation branch. We
count this as one fallback and include the box in the tracking
metrics.
WildDet3D-style filtering.
For the observation-only
Kalman rows in Table 3, we apply the same causal filter
to boxes from WildDet3D, OVMono3D-Lift and MoCA4D.
We adapt the WildDet3D demo [19] to the measured time
between observations.

the filter advances the center by ∆t v and carries s and v
forward. Every two raw frames it updates the state from the
observed center and dimensions. The first box sets c and s,
with v = 0.

D. Full-rollout object motion
Long-horizon propagation.
MoCA4D propagates each
cuboid by composing the predicted camera and object mo-
tions using only current and past frames. With a ground-truth
reset every eight transitions, it leads on all four MOVi-F met-

highest 3D IoU (0.5683) and the lowest inter-frame object ro-

SM4RT yields lower corner errors on full rollouts.

I. Star Optimization analyses
I.1. History and filtering in box refinement
One or three history edges.
We use three history edges
to stabilize the depth estimate along the observed viewing
ray. Each edge propagates a different past box to the current
frame. With one edge, the most recent box carries all the
historical weight. On AV2 and ADT, three edges lower
center-motion error by about 3% relative to one (Table S13).
One edge, in turn, gives 34–37% lower rotation-motion error
than three. When rotation is the priority, the same trained
model can use one edge at inference.
Dynamics-aware Kalman update.
Star treats a box prop-
agated by learned motion as a second estimate of the pose
seen in the current frame. Our Kalman variant uses the same
idea in a recursive filter. It propagates the last corrected box
and updates its depth and orientation with the current obser-
vation. On the observed ray, z+ = z−+ K(zobs −z−). We
normalize the measurement variance to R = 1. At regular
intervals, a process variance of Q = 1/6 gives a steady-state
gain of 1/3, matching Star’s observation weight for depth.
Lowering Q to 1/24 reduces the gain to 0.184. This gives
the lowest center-motion error on both datasets, although
AV2 absolute center error rises from 6.265 to 6.512 m (Ta-
ble S13).
